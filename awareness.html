Symptom Awareness

Understanding your body's signals is an important part of reproductive health awareness. Learn why recognising symptoms, paying attention to changes, and seeking appropriate healthcare advice matter.

Understanding Symptom Patterns

Awareness of reproductive health symptoms is important because changes in the menstrual cycle and other symptoms can sometimes indicate an underlying health condition.

Symptoms associated with PCOS and Endometriosis can vary in type and severity. Some symptoms can also overlap with other health conditions. For this reason, symptoms should be observed as patterns rather than considered in isolation.

Recognising Changes in Menstrual Health

Changes in menstrual patterns can provide important information about reproductive health.

Irregular menstrual periods can occur with PCOS, while Endometriosis is commonly associated with painful menstrual periods and pelvic pain.

Changes that are persistent, recurrent, or significantly different from an individual's usual menstrual pattern should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional.

Recognising Pain That Interferes With Daily Activities

Pain should receive particular attention when it becomes severe or interferes with normal daily activities.

Endometriosis-related pain can occur during menstruation or at other times in the menstrual cycle. Pain can also occur during bowel movements, urination, or sexual intercourse.

Severe or recurring pain should not simply be dismissed as normal menstrual discomfort.

Recognising Cyclical Symptoms

Some symptoms can follow a recurring pattern linked to the menstrual cycle.

Endometriosis can cause cyclical symptoms, including pelvic symptoms and, in some cases, symptoms occurring outside the pelvic region.

Recognising when symptoms appear, disappear, or become worse can provide useful information during medical assessment.

Monitoring Symptoms

Keeping a symptom diary can help identify patterns over time.

The record can include:

  • The type of symptom experienced
  • When the symptom occurs
  • How frequently it occurs
  • Whether it is associated with menstruation
  • Whether it affects normal daily activities

A symptom diary can provide useful information when discussing symptoms with a healthcare professional.

When Professional Assessment Is Important

Professional medical assessment is recommended when symptoms are persistent, severe, recurrent, or interfere with normal daily activities.

Examples include persistent pelvic pain, severe menstrual pain, painful urination or bowel movements, significant changes in menstrual patterns, or symptoms associated with difficulty becoming pregnant.

Early recognition of concerning symptoms can encourage appropriate medical assessment and help prevent symptoms from being overlooked.

Important Reminder

Symptom awareness does not replace medical diagnosis.

The presence of a particular symptom does not confirm PCOS, Endometriosis, or any other condition. A qualified healthcare professional should assess persistent or concerning symptoms and determine whether further evaluation is required.
